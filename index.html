Shared learning loop

Watch. Understand. Try. Change.

Planned lessons will use videos where Ren Gill explains his creative process. Questions will point to a checked timestamp. Practice will use our own exercises or verified public-domain material with permitted recordings.

  1. Watch or read: explore one authorized source at your own pace.
  2. Ask what it establishes: separate the artist's explanation from our interpretation.
  3. Try one idea: play, listen, record, or code a short exercise.
  4. Change and reflect: make one adjustment, describe it, and optionally save your work.

“My responses are limited to this source.”

“Not stated in this source” can be a correct answer. The guide will cite its evidence rather than inventing an artist's intentions. No source-checked quiz or conversational bot is available yet.

Why Tarantula and Inpatient?

The project grew from a fan chart for “Tarantula,” by Ren and Chris Webby. Its many paths now connect music, learning, coding and play. We design for different attention and access needs without asking people to disclose a diagnosis.

For contributors

Finish one useful learning loop

Help someone move from a musical idea to a small working result. An original synthetic-tone exercise now provides editable note data, timed and untimed practice, and local chart export. The next lesson milestone remains one verified source and three evidence-backed questions.

  • Musicians: check the exercise, explanations, and musical usefulness.
  • Coders: connect the lesson, chart editing, local export, and calm feedback.
  • Players and accessibility reviewers: test touch, keyboard, readable steps, focus, and recovery.

Use the gear you have: DJ2GO2, Tula, Shure and AirPods

Learn a pad/button in the MIDI exercise. Record original material with Tula in your chosen app, then bring a file to Creator OS practice. Shure and AirPods are listening routes; Apple accessibility settings remain under your control.

Equipment, AirPods accessibility and physical test guide. Input hardware, iPad/browser support and latency still require your test evidence. No new backend, purchase or microphone permission is required.

Cyber-G: play along first, validate input separately

Cyber-G can be an optional instrument alongside the lesson. USB audio and MIDI note events are different capabilities. Model, module, firmware, receiving app, and browser all need checking before automatic scoring or control is promised.

Start with GarageBand recording and local export, then test audio and keyboard MIDI separately. Safari browser MIDI access is not established by MIDI working in a native app.

Optional tools: notation, sound and controllers

MuseScore Studio supports the notation route; MuseHub offers optional sounds and effects. Desktop plugins are not automatically iPad-compatible. Use built-in sounds before adding dependencies.

Sonic Pi MIDI and OSC can connect controllers to sounds. Sonic Pi Web does not provide MIDI in Safari or browser OSC; use a supported app/environment for those experiments.

Legacy Clone Hero templates

song.ini and notes.chart remain unverified source templates. They are not a finished playable pack and supply no audio or artist permission. Validate metadata, timing, chart content and rights before reuse.

Clone Hero offers optional controller rhythm practice. Moonscraper edits charts. Game scores do not establish instrumental technique or musical understanding.

Choice, evidence and care

Pause, replay, skip or change paths. No forced streaks, required rankings, or personal health disclosures. No accounts, forms sending data, or analytics are added to this page; external tools have their own policies.

Keep practice and recording local first. Audio.com is an optional sharing route after reviewing credits, permissions and publication settings. An unlisted recording is still accessible to anyone with its link.

Publicly viewable music is not public domain. Composition, arrangement, recording, chart and video rights need separate consideration. MIT covers original project material we can license; it does not license artist works or third-party software.
