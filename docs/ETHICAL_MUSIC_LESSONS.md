# Ethical music lessons with rhythm-game practice

Status: proposal, not an implemented lesson system or validated teaching intervention.

This is a Loptr Lab music education project, separate from PIXIE. It adopts the same commitments to accessibility, consent, privacy, evidence limits, human accountability, and voluntary participation. This document states those commitments directly; it does not claim certification against an unreviewed PIXIE policy.

## Lesson structure

1. Watch an official or authorized video where Ren Gill explains a creative decision. Initially provide a source link and timestamp; use an official embed only when embedding is permitted.
2. Answer original questions about what that specific source establishes. Every factual answer needs a supporting timestamp and a checked explanation.
3. Practice the concept through a rights-cleared MIDI/MusicXML keyboard exercise in Synthesia, or an independently authored Clone Hero rhythm chart. Use verified public-domain material and permitted arrangements; any distributed recording also needs its own rights basis.
4. Reflect through an untimed text, listening, tapping, or discussion exercise. Gameplay is optional.

Potential topics include pulse, subdivision, syncopation, repetition, contrasting sections, and arrangement. These are candidate topics, not claims about what Ren says in any particular video. Select the source before writing the lesson.

Clone Hero supplies timing practice. The companion lesson supplies explanations and questions. Five-fret controller mappings do not teach actual guitar fingering, pitch, or harmony by themselves. Scores measure performance on the chart, not musical comprehension, health, or personal worth.

Clone Hero custom songs require a notes.chart or notes.mid, an audio file, and song.ini. Compatibility with other programs must be checked separately.

## Tool selection

Synthesia is the preferred candidate for a first keyboard lesson: its documented features include waiting for the correct note in melody practice, practicing hands separately, optional notation, and loading MIDI files. Its content guide also describes MusicXML. Check the installed version's actual import support and behavior.

Clone Hero remains an optional rhythm-game route. Neither application supplies the source-based video questions or explanations; those belong in our companion lesson.

Use an independently authored exercise illustrating the verified concept, not an unlicensed transcription of Ren's composition. A MIDI file is not automatically rights-free. Synthesia is third-party software: distribute our permitted lesson files, not the application, and do not promise free access to paid features. Check iPad import, input devices, accessibility, cost, and privacy before choosing it for a learner. Falling notes alone do not establish theory understanding or sound instrumental technique.

## Limited responses

The lesson's answer key, and any future assistant, must stay within the selected sources.

Use: "My responses are limited to this source. This video does not establish that answer."

"Not stated in this source" can be a correct quiz answer. Do not use it as an arbitrary refusal or punish uncertainty. Distinguish:
- what Ren explicitly says;
- what a learner can observe in the performance;
- our interpretation;
- general music instruction from a separately cited source.

Do not impersonate Ren, clone his voice, invent private intentions, or imply his participation or endorsement. No generative assistant is required for the pilot.

## Rights and provenance

Publicly viewable is not public domain. A composition, lyrics, arrangement, recording, video, and third-party chart can have different rights.

- Verify public-domain status for the intended distribution jurisdictions and the specific edition or arrangement. Record the evidence.
- Prefer a newly made recording of a verified public-domain composition, with explicit contributor permission.
- For licensed material, record the license, attribution requirements, permitted adaptations and distribution, and any restrictions. CC-licensed material is not necessarily public domain.
- Keep Ren's process videos as credited external references initially. Do not package their audio, video, lyrics, stems, or a transcription of his music without an appropriate rights basis and review.
- Educational or noncommercial purpose does not automatically establish fair use.
- Buying or streaming a song does not by itself establish permission to distribute it in a lesson pack.
- The repository's MIT license covers only original project material we can license. It does not grant rights in artist works, third-party charts, or Clone Hero itself.
- The existing Tarantula files are a separate, unverified template, not a rights-cleared public-domain lesson or finished playable chart.

For each asset record: title, creator, source URL, edition, composition rights, arrangement rights, recording rights, applicable territory, permission/license evidence, attribution, permitted use, reviewer, and review date. An unresolved field blocks distribution of that asset.

## Accessibility, consent, and privacy

- Provide readable instructions and an equivalent untimed learning route that does not require a note highway, color discrimination, rapid input, or a guitar controller.
- Provide checked captions or an authorized accessible source alternative; do not claim screen-reader access or iPad compatibility before testing.
- Allow pause, replay, skip, and withdrawal. Avoid mandatory streaks, public rankings, and pressure to finish.
- Give content notes when relevant; do not require learners to disclose illness, trauma, or other personal information.
- No account or telemetry is needed for the first pilot. Any later progress storage should be optional, local where practical, exportable, and deletable.
- Explain that external video players and game services have their own data practices. Review the actual version's online/leaderboard settings before using it with learners; do not promise the game is telemetry-free.
- Obtain separate, specific consent before recording, publishing, or sharing a playtest. State purpose, retention, deletion options, and any limits on withdrawal after publication.
- Sponsorship does not buy access, approvals, authority, or contributor status. Existing mission and participation terms apply.

## Smallest pilot and acceptance checks

The first pilot consists of one verified process video, three timestamp-supported questions, one short rights-cleared keyboard exercise (or rhythm chart), and one accessible alternative exercise.

Before calling it ready:
1. A human checks the video, question wording, answer evidence, and distinction between facts and interpretation.
2. Rights records are complete for every distributed asset.
3. The exercise imports and plays in the named Synthesia version (or the song pack scans and plays in Clone Hero); note mapping, timing, and difficulty are checked by a human.
4. A learner can explain the target concept without relying on a score.
5. The alternative learning route is usable; actual device/input and accessibility checks are recorded.
6. A consent-based playtest documents what worked, what failed, and what remains unverified.

No source video, public-domain composition, recording, permissions, device validation, or completed pilot is established by this proposal.
